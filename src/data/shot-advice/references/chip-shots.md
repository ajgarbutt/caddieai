# Chip shots

## Standard chip shot

**Distance:** 10–50 yards total to hole.
**When to use:** Default chip around the green with a clear path onto the green — fairway, fringe, or light rough (sitting up reasonably well). Includes short-sided pins and shots with plenty of green. **Not** for a major hazard that must be carried (bunker, water).
**When it can't be used:** Ball buried in thick rough (use thick-rough chip). Major obstacle that must be carried — bunker, water, or a steep bank that needs a high soft landing (use high soft chip). Hardpan/tight bare may need the tight-lie adjustments below.

**Club selection:**
- SW: less green to work with, or more carry needed over fringe/rough before the green
- PW: ground is flat and slightly more rollout is acceptable
- Gap wedge: more carry than PW but less loft than SW
- More green ahead = slightly less loft is fine (still the same stroke); less green = more loft

Choose loft for how much green you have — not a high flop.

**Set-up:** Ball in line with the **trail foot**. Feet about **1 clubhead** apart. ~60% weight on lead foot. Face square.
**Technique:** Putting-stroke feel — soft hands and soft arms. Motion from the **body turning** — no independent arm swing and no high wrist hinge. Quiet short stroke; keep loft on the club; no big shaft lean. Imagine you must keep the **clubhead below hip height**. Land it on the green and let it release a little.
**Swing thought:** Soft hands, body turns the club — like a putt with loft.

## High soft chip

**Distance:** 10–30 yards.
**When to use:** **Only** when an obstacle between ball and green must be carried and the ball needs to land softly — typically a bunker, water, or a steep bank/lip. Ball on fairway, fringe or clean light rough.
**When it can't be used:** Clear path onto the green — use a **standard chip** instead. Tight bare lie / hardpan (wedge bounce thins easily). Deep rough or wet turf. Do **not** default to this shot just because the flag is close to the fringe.

**Club selection:**
- SW: safer option for most forced carries
- LW: only when maximum height and softest landing are truly required to clear the obstacle

**Set-up / stance:** Stand slightly **farther** from the ball than for a standard chip, with hands **lower**. Weight biased slightly on the lead foot.
**Grip:** Extremely light — about **3 out of 10**. Feel the club mainly in the fingers, not the thumbs.
**Backswing:** Do not shut the face or roll the club too far inside. Hinge the wrists so the **toe points vertically** at the top of the backswing.
**Downswing & release:** Wider swing arc and fluid body rotation — do not actively manipulate the hands through impact. Let the chest rotate fully through to the target. Trust gravity to let the club **slide beneath the ball**, using the club’s bounce. Do not scoop.
**Swing thought:** Light grip, toe up in the backswing, chest turns through — let the bounce slide under it.

## Chip from thick rough

**Distance:** 5–30 yards.
**When to use:** Ball sitting down in thick rough near the green. Shot chosen because of the lie, not the rollout requirements.
**When it can't be used:** Ball is badly buried with very little room for error — in that case simply getting the ball onto the green is the goal. Do not attempt a delicate shot.

**Club selection:**
- SW: usually safest
- LW: only when more height is genuinely required and player can fully commit

**Set-up:** Ball centre to slightly back of centre. Slightly narrower stance. 60–70% weight on lead foot.
**Technique:** Steeper, firmer strike through the grass. Expect less spin and more rollout than the same shot from fairway. Do not decelerate — commit through.
**Swing thought:** Accelerate through the grass.

## Chip from a tight lie

**Distance:** 5–25 yards.
**When to use:** Short grass, hard ground, closely mown turf or a bare patch around the green. Enough green for some rollout.
**When it can't be used:** Avoid a high-lofted open-face shot from a tight lie unless absolutely necessary. Very little margin for error — wedge bounce makes these shots easy to thin.

**Why wedges are risky:** Sand and lob wedges have bounce on the sole to stop them digging in soft ground. On hardpan, that bounce can rebound off the firm surface and skim into the equator of the ball (a thin/blade). Opening the face increases effective bounce and makes this worse.

**Club selection:**
- PW: often the safest wedge choice (less bounce, still some loft)
- 9 iron or 8 iron: usually the best option on hardpan — gentle, controlled stroke
- SW: only when carry is genuinely required; keep the face square, do not open it
- LW / high soft chip: avoid from hardpan

**Set-up:** Ball slightly back of centre (toward trail foot). Narrow stance, weight slightly forward, hands just ahead of ball.
**Technique:** Focus on brushing the turf after the ball. Compact motion. Do not try to lift the ball or dig under it.
**Swing thought:** Pick it clean.

## Downhill chip

**Distance:** 5–25 yards.
**When to use:** Downhill slope between ball and hole. Less rollout needed than distance suggests — the slope does the work.
**When it can't be used:** Taking too little loft when the slope will send the ball running away strongly. Do not attack a tight pin if there is trouble beyond it.

**Club selection:**
- SW: usually safest for limiting rollout
- LW: when slope is severe and there is very little room beyond the landing area

**Set-up:** Ball slightly forward of centre. Weight on lead foot. Match shoulders to slope where possible.
**Technique:** Soft, controlled swing. Land the ball earlier than instinct suggests and let the slope do the work.
**Swing thought:** Land it early and let it release.

---

## Uphill chip

**Distance:** 5–30 yards.
**When to use:** Uphill slope between ball and hole. Ball will stop more quickly than usual.
**When it can't be used:** Avoid trying to run a low chip all the way up a steep slope unless there is plenty of room and no trouble behind the hole.

**Club selection:**
- PW: lower running option on a gentle slope
- SW: more carry and softer landing
- Gap wedge: useful middle option

**Set-up:** Ball centre to slightly forward of centre. Weight slightly on lead foot. Let body match the uphill slope.
**Technique:** Use enough swing to carry the ball onto the slope. Expect the ball to stop more quickly than usual.
**Swing thought:** Carry it onto the slope.
