# Approach shots

The pin is almost never the correct target for a high handicapper. Before every approach, identify: where is the danger, what is the safe side, and what is the biggest landing zone away from trouble?

---

## Safe centre-green approach

**Distance:** 70–180 yards.
**When to use:** Default approach shot when no specific hazard forces a different target. Aim for the safe middle of the green rather than the pin.
**When it can't be used:** Adjust if a major hazard covers the middle, or if the green shape makes another side clearly safer.

**Club selection:** Choose the club that carries comfortably to the safe middle area based on the player's stock yardages. Use a 7/10 strike as the standard — not a perfect strike.
**Technique:** Solid contact. Aim away from the biggest danger zone.
**Swing thought:** Middle of the green.

---

## Approach with water behind the green

**Distance:** 70–180 yards.
**When to use:** Water is behind or long of the green. The safe miss is short, not long.

**Club selection:** Take enough club to reach the safe front or middle of the green. Do not choose a club that makes the water a likely miss if struck well. Short is safe — a chip back from the fringe beats a drop from the hazard.
**Technique:** Aim for the front or centre of the green. Accept a longer putt over bringing the water into play.
**Swing thought:** Short is safe.

---

## Approach with bunker protecting the pin

**Distance:** 70–180 yards.
**When to use:** A bunker sits between the golfer and the pin, or directly guards the flag.

**Club selection:** Choose the club that reaches the safest section of the green — not based solely on pin distance if the safe target is elsewhere on the green.
**Technique:** Aim at the safe side of the green, away from the bunker. Focus on solid contact.
**Swing thought:** Play away from trouble.

---

## Approach from light rough

**Distance:** 70–180 yards.
**When to use:** Ball sitting reasonably well in light rough. Can still advance to the green but expect some loss of control and spin.

**Club selection:** Often take one more club than the equivalent fairway shot, depending on the lie. Assess whether it is a flyer lie (ball sitting up — may go further) or a sitting-down lie (less distance).
**Ball position:** Normal to slightly back of normal.
**Technique:** Strike through the grass. Expect less spin and potentially more rollout after landing. Do not attack a tight pin where spin is needed to stop the ball.
**Swing thought:** Commit through the grass.

---

## Approach from thick rough

**Distance:** 50–150 yards.
**When to use:** Ball sitting down in thick rough. May not be able to reach the green — assess honestly.
**When it can't be used:** If the ball is badly buried and the carry over hazards is not achievable, advance the ball to a better position rather than trying for the green.

**Club selection:** Use more loft than the distance would normally require. A wedge or short iron is often safer than a hybrid or long iron from thick rough.
**Ball position:** Centre to slightly back of centre.
**Technique:** Committed swing through the grass. Expect significantly less distance and spin. Aim for the safest broad target on the green.
**Swing thought:** Escape first.
