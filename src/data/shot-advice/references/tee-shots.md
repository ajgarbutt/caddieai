# Tee shots

**Club selection rule:** Only recommend clubs from the player's bag with their saved carry distances. The examples below describe shot shapes and strategy — do not quote generic driver or 3-wood yardages unless that club is in the bag with a saved distance.

Apply the relevant section below based on what is in the bag and what the hole demands.

---

## Safe driver tee shot

**When to use:** Longer par 4s and par 5s where the fairway is wide enough that a driver mishit stays in play — only if the player carries a driver.
**When it can't be used:** OB, water, trees or a very narrow landing area make a large miss too costly. In those cases use a positional tee shot instead.

**Set-up:** Ball inside lead heel. Stance wider than shoulder width. Tee ball so roughly half of it sits above the top of the driver face.
**Technique:** Balanced, controlled swing at around 80–85% effort. Not maximum distance — a controlled driver that finds the fairway is worth far more than a big drive in the rough.
**Swing thought:** Balanced finish.

---

## Positional tee shot

**When to use:** Short par 4s, tight holes, doglegs, or any hole where the longest club brings trouble into play. Rewards accuracy and placement over distance.
**When it can't be used:** Avoid if the chosen club leaves an impossible second shot or does not reach a safe landing area.

**Club selection:** Choose the longest club from the bag that reaches the widest safe part of the fairway without bringing major trouble into play. Favour hybrids, fairway woods, or long irons the player actually carries.

**Ball position:** Slightly forward of centre for fairway woods or hybrids. Around centre for a long iron.
**Technique:** Aim at the widest safe target. Smooth swing. Keeping the ball in play is the only objective.
**Swing thought:** Fairway first.

---

## Tee shot with OB on one side

**When to use:** Out of bounds is clearly in play from the tee — left or right.
**When it can't be used:** Do not aim near OB simply because it gives a shorter line to the green. The stroke-and-distance penalty is the worst outcome in golf.

**Strategy:**
- Tee up on the same side as the OB — this opens the entire fairway as your target
- Aim away from OB, using the full width of the fairway as margin
- If the player's typical miss goes toward OB, take a shorter club

**Club selection:** The club that gives the best chance of staying away from OB — may be 3 hybrid, 3 wood or driver depending on hole width and the player's normal miss direction.
**Technique:** Aim conservatively. Do not chase maximum distance.
**Swing thought:** Take out the big number.

---

## Tee shot into wind

**When to use:** Wind is noticeably into the player.

**Key point:** Do not swing harder to fight the wind. Swinging harder increases spin, which makes the ball climb and balloon — it makes the wind affect it more, not less.

**Club selection:** Enough club for the safe landing area. A more controlled shorter club can be better on a narrow hole into wind.
**Technique:** Smooth, controlled swing at 75–85% effort. Stable base. Balanced finish.
**Swing thought:** Smooth beats hard.

---

## Iron tee shot for accuracy

**When to use:** Narrow fairways, hazards or OB close to the landing area, short par 4s where position matters more than distance, or when the player is not comfortable hitting driver or fairway woods consistently.

This is an underused and underrated option. A safe 7 or 8 iron in the fairway is worth far more than a driver in the rough or OB.

**Club selection:**
- 9 iron: when the hole is very tight or simply getting in play is the goal
- 8 iron: strong default for accuracy-focused tee shots
- 7 iron: when slightly more distance is needed but control is still the priority

Do not automatically choose the longest iron. Choose the one the player can strike confidently and reliably.

**When it can't be used:** Avoid if the chosen iron leaves an unrealistic second shot — e.g. needing to carry a large hazard or reach a green well beyond the remaining distance.

**Set-up:** Normal iron stance. Tee ball very low, just above the ground, so a normal iron swing can be made.
**Technique:** Smooth, balanced swing. Aim for the widest safe part of the fairway — even if that means aiming slightly away from the ideal line to the green.
**Swing thought:** Find the fairway first.
