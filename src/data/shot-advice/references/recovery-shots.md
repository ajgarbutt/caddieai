# Recovery shots

## Punch under trees

**Distance:** 30–150 yards depending on available space.
**When to use:** Low gap under branches with clear ground for the ball to run after landing.
**When it can't be used:** Ball buried in thick rough. No safe gap. The forward route brings water, OB or more trees too closely into play — in that case chip out sideways.

**Club selection:**
- 6, 7 or 8 iron are common
- More loft if ball needs to stop sooner
- Less loft only if there is plenty of room and a very low flight is required

**Set-up:** Ball back of centre. Narrow stance, weight slightly forward.
**Technique:** Grip down, compact swing, finish low. Keep the ball under the branches. The goal is position, not perfection.
**Swing thought:** Low and safe.

## Chip out sideways

**Distance:** 5–30 yards — only enough to return the ball to safety.
**When to use:** Behind trees, in deep rough, near trouble, or without a safe forward route. This is the correct play for a high-handicapper whenever the forward route is too risky. Do not be embarrassed to take it.
**When it can't be used:** Use it whenever the forward route is genuinely risky. There are very few situations where a high handicapper should not take this option when in serious trouble.

**Club selection:**
- PW or 9 iron for a controlled escape
- SW if ball is sitting in heavier grass and needs more loft

**Set-up:** Ball centre to slightly back of centre. Narrow stable stance, weight slightly forward.
**Technique:** Pick the safest available target — even if it means moving away from the hole. Controlled swing. Get the ball back into play.
**Swing thought:** Take your medicine.

## Advance from thick rough

**Distance:** 30–150 yards depending on how thick the grass is and what lies ahead.
**When to use:** Ball in thick rough but a reasonably clear route forward exists. Priority is advancing the ball without a worse miss.
**When it can't be used:** Ball buried, rough is very heavy, or hazards ahead make a poor strike from rough too costly. In that case, chip out sideways.

**Club selection:**
- Take more loft than the distance would normally suggest
- 7, 8 or 9 iron is often safer than a fairway wood or hybrid from rough
- Use a wedge if the grass is very thick and simply escaping is the realistic goal

**Set-up:** Ball centre to slightly back of centre. Widen stance slightly for balance. Firm grip. Weight slightly forward.
**Technique:** Committed swing through the grass. Expect less distance and less spin than from the fairway.
**Swing thought:** Get through the grass.
