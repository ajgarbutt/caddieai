# Shot factors

## Lie

The lie is one of the most important factors in club selection. Always establish it before recommending a shot.

**Fairway** — normal distance and shot shape. Full swing available.

**Light rough (ball sitting up)** — a "flyer" lie. Grass gets between the clubface and ball, reducing spin. The ball will fly further and run more than expected. Consider taking one less club. Less control over the shot — avoid going at a tight pin from a flyer lie.

**Light rough (ball sitting down)** — expect 5–10% distance loss. Full swing available but less spin, so allow for more run on landing.

**Thick rough** — significant distance loss (10–30% depending on depth). The priority is to escape, not advance the ball the full distance. Take a more lofted club and swing through the grass. From very thick rough, even getting the ball back to the fairway is a good outcome. Never try to hit a low punch or long iron from thick rough.

**Tight/hardpan** — ball sits low with little grass beneath it. More prone to thin strikes. Play a more conservative, controlled swing. Prefer one extra club and a smooth swing over an aggressive full swing.

Wedges are especially tricky here because of **bounce** — the angled sole under the clubface that stops the club digging into soft turf. On hardpan there is nothing soft to dig into, so that bounce can skip/bounce off the surface and cause a thin (bladed) strike. Avoid high-bounce wedges (SW/LW) for full or aggressive swings from hardpan unless the lie is genuinely clean and you must use loft. Around the green on hardpan, favour a lower-bounce club (PW, 9i, or 8i) with a gentle stroke over an open-faced soft wedge.

**Divot** — ball is sitting below the level of the turf. Strike down more steeply. Expect reduced distance and a lower ball flight. Take more club and aim for the middle of the green — this is not a time to attack a pin.

**Pine needles / leaves** — ball can move at address. Take care at address. The ball may come out hotter than expected (less friction). Assess carefully.

## Stance (ball position relative to feet)

**Flat** — normal. No adjustments needed.

**Ball above feet** — the club is effectively more upright, which promotes a draw/hook (ball goes left for a right-handed player). Aim right of target. Swing shorter and more controlled — a full swing amplifies the curve. Expect slightly less distance.

**Ball below feet** — the club is flatter, promoting a fade/slice (ball goes right for a right-hander). Aim left of target. More prone to thin strikes as it's harder to get down to the ball. Take one extra club and make solid contact the priority over distance.

**Uphill lie** — effectively adds loft to the club (a 7 iron plays like an 8 or 9). Ball will fly higher and shorter. Take 1–2 extra clubs depending on severity. Ball tends to pull left — aim slightly right. Weight naturally falls to the back foot; fight this by setting up with weight forward.

**Downhill lie** — reduces loft (a 7 iron plays more like a 6). Ball will fly lower and run more on landing. Take 1 less club. Ball tends to push/fade right — aim left. Harder to get solid contact — don't try to scoop. Let the club do the work and accept the lower trajectory.

## Wind

High handicappers are more affected by wind than low handicappers because the ball flight is higher and slower. Apply these adjustments:

| Condition | Adjustment |
|-----------|------------|
| Light headwind (5–10mph) | 1 club more |
| Medium headwind (10–20mph) | 1–2 clubs more |
| Strong headwind (20mph+) | 2–3 clubs more |
| Light tailwind (5–10mph) | 1 club less |
| Medium tailwind (10–20mph) | 1–2 clubs less |
| Crosswind | Aim into the wind to allow for drift |

**Into the wind**: Take more club and swing easier. Trying to hit harder increases spin, which makes wind affect the ball more. A smooth, controlled swing into the wind is always better than swinging out of your shoes.

**Short approach / pitch / chip in wind (~30–80 yards):** Prefer a **lower-lofted club with a shorter swing** so the ball flies lower and is less caught by the wind. Still scale the swing to that club's stock carry — do not take a long-carry club and make a big swing at a short target.

**Downwind**: Take less club, but expect the ball to run further on landing — the green may be firmer if downwind, and the ball won't stop as quickly. Allow for extra run.

**Crosswind**: Aim into the wind and let it bring the ball back. Right-to-left wind: aim right and let it drift in. Left-to-right: aim left. Don't try to curve the ball against the wind — it will exaggerate the miss.

## Hazards

Before every shot, identify: what is the worst place to miss, and what is the safe miss?

**Water** — the most costly hazard (stroke + distance penalty). Never recommend a carry over water unless:
1. The player can carry it comfortably on a 7/10 strike (not their best)
2. The penalty for laying up is not significantly better than going for it

When in doubt, lay up. A bogey from a lay-up beats a double from a watery grave.

**Bunkers** — less costly than water, but still a lost shot for most high handicappers. Identify whether the bunker is a greenside bunker (difficult to escape with an accurate shot) or a fairway bunker (manageable, but distance is lost). Aim away from bunkers. If a bunker guards one side of the green, aim to the opposite side.

**Out of bounds** — treat the same as water. If OB is in play, take it out of play. Club down, aim away, accept the shorter shot.

**Trees** — can be played through if there's a realistic gap, but assess honestly. For a high handicapper, the chance of threading a gap in trees is low. Usually better to pitch out sideways and accept the lost shot.

**Safe miss principle**: Before every approach shot, ask "if I miss this by 10 yards left or right, long or short, where do I end up?" Aim to make the most likely miss a recoverable one. If missing short-right goes in a bunker, aim left-centre. If missing long means chipping back from a simple fringe, don't be afraid to be past the pin.

## Lay-up decisions

Lay up when:
- The carry is not achievable on a 7/10 strike
- The penalty for missing is severe (water, OB)
- There is a clearly better yardage to lay up to (e.g., a full PW rather than a partial from 110 yards)

When laying up, have a specific target in mind — not just "short of the water" but "leave myself a full 9 iron." Work backwards from the shot you want to play next.

## Chipping and pitching

Detailed chip and pitch shot types — standard chip, high soft chip, tight lie, thick rough, uphill/downhill, and all pitch variations — are in `chip-shots.md` and `pitch-shots.md`. The summary principle:

### Decision: standard chip vs high soft

| Factor | Standard chip | High soft chip |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Lie needed | Fairway, fringe, or light rough | Fairway or clean light rough |
| Path to green | Clear — no bunker/water to carry | Obstacle must be carried |
| Clubs used | PW, gap, SW (loft for green available) | SW, LW |
| Hazards to carry | Fringe/rough/uneven ground only | Bunker, water, or steep bank |

**Default to the standard chip** whenever there is a clear path onto the green. **High soft chip only** when a bunker, water, or similar obstacle must be carried.

---

### Standard chip shot

**When to use:**
- 10–50 yards total distance to the hole
- Clear path onto the green — **no** bunker or water to carry
- Ball on the fairway, fringe or light rough (sitting up reasonably well)

**Club selection:**
- SW: less green to work with, or more carry over fringe/rough
- PW: flatter ground, slightly more rollout OK
- Gap wedge: middle option
- More green → slightly less loft; less green → more loft — same stroke either way

**Set-up:**
- Ball in line with the **trail foot**
- Feet about **1 clubhead** apart
- ~60% weight on lead foot
- Face square

**Technique:**
- Putting-stroke feel: soft hands, soft arms
- Motion from the **body turning** — no independent arm swing, no high wrist hinge
- Quiet short stroke; keep loft; no big shaft lean
- Keep the **clubhead below hip height**

**Swing thought:** soft hands, body turns the club — like a putt with loft.

---

### High soft chip

**When to use:** Only when a bunker, water, or steep bank/lip **must be carried** and the ball needs to land softly. Not for a short-sided pin alone.

**When not to use:** Clear path to the green — use a standard chip. Hardpan. Deep rough.

**Club selection:** SW safer; LW only if maximum height is required to clear the obstacle.

**Set-up:** Stand slightly farther from the ball than a standard chip; hands lower; weight slightly on lead foot.
**Grip:** Extremely light (~3/10), club felt in the fingers not the thumbs.
**Backswing:** Hinge so the toe points vertically at the top — do not shut the face or roll too far inside.
**Downswing:** Wider arc, fluid body rotation; chest turns fully through; trust gravity and bounce to slide the club under the ball — do not scoop or hand-manipulate through impact.

**Swing thought:** light grip, toe up in the backswing, chest turns through — let the bounce slide under it.

---

### From tight lies around the green

- Prefer PW or a short iron (9i/8i) over an open-faced sand/lob wedge — bounce on SW/LW is a liability on hardpan
- Use a gentle, controlled stroke; pick it clean
- A bellied wedge (hitting the equator of the ball with the leading edge) is a useful recovery when the ball is sitting tight against the fringe — it runs like a putt

## Tee shots

**Identify the optimal side of the fairway first** — where do you need to be to have the best angle into the green? Play to that side.

**Identify the trouble** — where is OB, water, thick rough? Aim away from it. If trouble is on the right, tee up on the right side of the tee box and aim left. This opens up the entire fairway as a margin for error.

**Width of the landing zone** — aim for the widest part of the fairway, not the narrow gap between hazards. Accept a longer approach from a wider landing zone over a shorter approach from trouble.

**3 hybrid off the tee** — at 200 yards carry, this club is accurate and reliable. On tight holes, it is always the right play. On par 5s, a 3 hybrid to the fairway followed by two more irons beats a hero shot that goes out of bounds.

---

## Approach shot target selection

The pin is almost never the correct target for a high handicapper. The correct target is determined by:

1. **Where is the danger?** — identify which sides of the green are trouble (bunkers, water, run-offs)
2. **Where is the safe miss?** — aim to make the likely miss a straightforward chip or pitch
3. **Where is the biggest safe area?** — aim for the largest portion of the green away from trouble
4. **Is the carry achievable?** — if the front of the green has trouble, make sure the carry is comfortably within range

**Pin positions to be extra cautious with:**
- Tucked behind a front bunker: always land past the bunker, accept being back of pin
- Cut tight to the edge of the green: aim for the centre, don't chase it
- Back of the green with a fall-off behind: aim front-centre, two-putt is fine

**Two putts from the middle of the green is a good outcome.** Never sacrifice that outcome by trying to get too close to a well-guarded pin.
